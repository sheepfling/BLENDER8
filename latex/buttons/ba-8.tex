\section*{Assembly description}
The BA-8 contains seven mutually interlocked speed-selection buttons, a momentary PULSE button, a separate STOP button and a panel power switch. Eight command-sense contacts serve selections 1--7 and PULSE. STOP and power are separate functions; neither consumes a command channel.

\begin{Notice}{STOP TAKES PRECEDENCE}
STOP dominates simultaneous speed and PULSE operation. A speed pressed while STOP is held is ignored. A PULSE held through STOP remains blocked after STOP release until PULSE is released and freshly pressed.
\end{Notice}
\section*{Panel power}
The rear switch contacts may make and break during the first 5 ms of an actuation. This contact envelope is not the downstream supply rise time, reset delay or clock startup time. The switch assembly provides a separate auxiliary indication for power qualification.

The panel power switch is mechanically independent of the command latches. Removing electrical power does not release a latched selection. A retained selection is present at the contacts when electrical sensing resumes; the assembly does not generate a new press-and-release sequence on power restoration.

\section*{Contact transition envelope}
Command contacts may make and break repeatedly during the first 5 ms after a mechanical transition. After that interval they assume the final state. The detailed sequence is not prescribed. The envelope applies to make and break transitions; it is not a fixed series of edges or a guaranteed number of transitions.

\section*{State at the assembly boundary}
The command contacts report closure to the common return, not a decoded operating request. The assembly does not assign a shaft speed, generate a drive waveform or maintain a display. Printed button legends identify the supplied panel controls.

A PULSE press operates its momentary contact subject to the specified transition envelope. There is no built-in minimum-hold timer or qualified-event output. Any minimum actuation duration accepted by the finished equipment is outside this assembly specification.
\section*{Sense-circuit failures}
A stuck-closed contact can indicate a selection without the associated mechanical latch. A stuck-open contact can conceal an actual selection. An electrical fault in a command-sense contact does not, by itself, change the underlying latch or the independent RUN\_PERMIT state.

Consequently, two speed contacts can read closed under wiring or contact faults even though the healthy interlock permits only one speed latch. Sequential observations during a transition can also contain samples taken from different mechanical states.

\section*{Bench acceptance}
With defined bias on the contacts, operate each latching selection and verify mutual release. Check PULSE both alone and with a selected speed. PULSE must leave the speed latch unchanged.

Hold PULSE, press STOP, then release STOP while continuing to hold PULSE. The suppressed PULSE command must not return. Release and freshly press PULSE to restore its normal momentary action. A speed press while STOP is held must not establish a latch.

Interrupt electrical power with a speed latched, then restore sensing. Verify that the mechanical selection is retained. Press STOP and verify release of all speed latches, the STOP\_N indication and removal of RUN\_PERMIT as separate observations.
