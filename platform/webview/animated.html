Start the controller: allow 2 seconds of cool logical time, hold STOP briefly and release, then select 1–7 or hold P. After a fault: remove its cause, let the measured temperature stay at or below 65°C for 2 seconds, tap STOP, release all commands, then select again. CK requires restoring the clock and MCU RESET.
Firmware LCD: 0 = stopped; 1–7 = selection; P = pulse. CK clock, WD watchdog, DM deadman, TS temperature signal, TH overtemperature, IF conflicting inputs, IL jar interlock, ST stalled shaft / missing tach. Faults latch until recovery. Probe and component bench do not implement these appliance codes.




Operation and execution boundaries
The page opens Selected firmware and starts logical time. The rear switch initially requests ON. RUN/PAUSE controls simulation time; POWER ON/OFF controls the physical supply. A loaded starter may still be unfinished. Pixel probe intentionally leaves the motor off. Component bench has no firmware; its MOTOR EXHIBIT is a finite electrical demonstration.
Hold P or Space; a STOP click completes a 50 ms press in logical time. Releasing outside the canvas still releases input; focus loss cancels GUI ownership immediately. Clicking another control or the debug panel keeps the simulation running. A hidden tab or unfocused window suspends pacing and releases held inputs; returning resumes pacing if RUN was selected. Hidden time is never caught up. There is no session duration limit. Simulation pause freezes the LCD and physics; electrical STOP removes drive while the shaft coasts. MCU RESET resets the controller; Restart session recreates the whole machine.
Motor and Thermal + Truth show the same C++ plant. The thermal page overlays actual ADC reads, sensor wire, case, sensor lag, local air, food and room. Food buttons replace the contents: room-temperature water (15% load, 25°C), frozen fruit (80%, -10°C), hot vegetables (45%, 80°C), or an empty jug. The drop animation is illustrative; the model uses lumped conduction and averaged load, without fluid dynamics or ice melting. Case +20°C and -20°C change only the motor case; the sensor responds with its modeled lag.
The bottom trace retains the latest contact transition and 10 ms after it, sampled at 100 µs, so bounce remains visible after it settles. BOUNCE cycles nominal 1.8 ms, 5 ms and off. The motor-power and jar-permission lamps are read-only indicators. JAM SHAFT is a fault fixture: the firmware must detect missing motion, remove drive and write ST. The host never manufactures LCD codes.
Supervision (U): watchdog and deadman enablement, locks, counters, service counts, deadman window and reboot history come directly from the MCU model. Reboot reasons are bit flags: POR 01, BOR 02, WDT 04, DMT 08, CLOCK 10, SOFT 20, EXTERNAL 40 (hex). History retains the last 16 events even after firmware clears the registers. MCU RESET preserves history; Restart session clears it. The production watchdog is fused on. Developers can build a firmware-controlled development fuse with --watchdog firmware; the page observes the uploaded image’s configuration. Deadman enablement is always firmware controlled until locked.
LCD BUS (L): the eight-bit data bus travels MCU to LCD; VBLANK travels LCD to MCU. The scopes show actual 16 ms scan / 4 ms blanking edges and the most recent DATA-write burst, with D7 through D0 and hexadecimal bytes. Pink bytes were dropped while BUSY. CPU, bench and DMA writes share the capture. Byte values are held between transaction markers for readability; electrical setup/hold timing is not modeled. Histories retain 64 DATA writes, 32 bus accesses and 32 blanking/reset edges, with lifetime counters. Observation never reads registers or advances time.
All drawing, hit testing, animations and physics are C++. The browser presents the RGBA framebuffer and forwards events. Histories retain ten logical minutes, without limiting execution. Missing/stale ADC reads remain explicit. Diagnostic controls and fixture commands are available at the original workbench. This browser is not a physical HWIL driver.
